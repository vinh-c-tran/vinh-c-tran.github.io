Purpose and Contribution

This project covers the wiring, assembly, and verification of a He4 transport probe. This probe enables cryogenic magnetotransport measurements of samples connected via a removable chip carrier.

My contribution to this project was to select the components, design the cold finger, design the wiring assembly, solder and assemble the wiring components, and perform validation testing. My other responsibilities here were to coordinate with other people who were responsible for other aspects of the wiring - break out box and break out box to probe cable to ensure pin out and mapping were consistent

Wiring Assembly Design Choices

The probe arrived partially wired from the top down to the wires near the sample location, in unterminated wires. The remaining work then is to wire these wires to a sample socket. Our design ended up being 1. Sample Socket: DIP-24 Socket as a versatile sample socket wired in twisted pairs 2. Sample stage on which the sample socket sits and allows for the sample/chip carrier to have thermal contact with a heater (for temperature control). 3. Intermediate connector: Micro d-sub connector that connects the probe wires to the sample socket

Sample Stage

I designed a sample stage that fit the dimensions of the probe with careful design to the probe dimensions. Design choice(s)/considerations: 1. Clearance for dip socket legs and wiring/soldering 2. Height of main protruding body to make good thermal contact with chip carrier 3. Overall height of body such that center of sample is at center of magnetic field 4. Material body is high thermal conductivity (Oxygen-free High Thermal Conductivity Copper) followed by gold plating 5. Circumference of the sample stage should match the circumference of the mount Subject to these constraints, I designed several prototypes in CAD before settling on a final design, each step of iteration by 3D printing a physical model for testing.

Electrical Isolation/Leakage

This checks the isolation of a pin from others or measures the unintended flow of current through other pins. This is done by applying a voltage to one pin and measuring the current to all other pins grounded without a sample connecting the pins. Ideally, the resistance here is as large as possible.
